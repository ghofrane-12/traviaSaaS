1. INTRODUCTION
===============================================================================
---
1.1. SCOPE OF THIS GUIDE
---
This guide is designed for **transport agents, travel professionals, and claims handlers** to:
✅ Understand **legal rights** of passengers during flight disruptions.
✅ Navigate **airline-specific policies** for delays, cancellations, and denied boarding.
✅ **Maximize compensation** for clients using regulatory frameworks (EU 261, US DOT, etc.).
✅ Handle **real-life scenarios** (strikes, weather, technical issues) with step-by-step solutions.
✅ Use **templates and checklists** to streamline the claims process.

> **Key Statistic (2025):**
> - **68% of flight disruptions** are due to airline operational issues (not "extraordinary circumstances").
> - **Only 37% of eligible passengers** claim compensation (IATA 2025).
> - **Average payout for EU claims:** €400 (AirHelp 2025).

---
1.2. KEY DEFINITIONS
---
| Term                     | Definition                                                                 |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Extraordinary Circumstance** | Events beyond the airline’s control (e.g., weather, strikes, political unrest). **Not eligible for compensation** in most regions. |
| **Operational Issue**    | Airline’s fault (e.g., crew shortage, technical problems, overbooking). **Eligible for compensation**. |
| **Denied Boarding**     | Passenger not allowed to board due to overbooking (even with a valid ticket). |
| **Missed Connection**    | Passenger misses a connecting flight due to a delay on the first leg.     |
| **Diversion**           | Flight lands at an unplanned airport due to emergency.                     |
| **Force Majeure**       | Unforeseeable events (e.g., natural disasters, wars). Exempts airlines from compensation. |

---
1.3. COMMON CAUSES OF FLIGHT DISRUPTIONS
---
| Cause                  | Compensation Eligible? | Notes                                  |
|------------------------|------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Crew Shortage**      | Yes                    | Airline’s operational issue.         |
| **Technical Problems** | Yes                    | Unless it’s a safety-related defect.  |
| **Strikes (Airline)**  | Yes (EU) / No (US)     | EU 261 covers airline staff strikes.  |
| **Weather**            | No                     | Considered "extraordinary circumstance." |
| **Air Traffic Control**| No                     | Usually out of airline’s control.      |
| **Overbooking**        | Yes                    | Always compensable under EU/US laws.   |
| **Medical Emergency**  | No                     | Onboard medical issues.               |
| **Political Unrest**   | No                     | Force majeure.                         |
| **Bird Strike**        | Sometimes             | Depends on airline’s prevention measures. |

2. LEGAL FRAMEWORK BY REGION

---
2.1. EUROPEAN UNION (REGULATION 261/2004)
---
**Applies to:**
- All flights **departing from an EU airport** (regardless of airline).
- Flights **arriving in the EU** on an **EU-based airline**.

**Compensation Eligibility:**
| Disruption Type       | Delay Duration | Compensation (€) | Notes                                  |
|-----------------------|-----------------|-------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Delay**             | 3+ hours        | 250–600           | Based on distance.                    |
| **Cancellation**      | <14 days notice | 250–600           | Unless "extraordinary circumstances." |
| **Denied Boarding**   | Any             | 250–600           | Always compensable.                   |

**Distance-Based Compensation:**
| Flight Distance       | Compensation (€) |
|-----------------------|-------------------|
| <1,500 km             | 250               |
| 1,500–3,500 km        | 400               |
| >3,500 km (EU flight) | 600               |

**Additional Rights:**
- **Meals/Refreshments:** Vouchers for delays >2 hours.
- **Accommodation:** Hotel + transport if overnight delay.
- **Re-routing:** Alternative flight or full refund.

> **Pro Tip for Agents:**
> - **Always check if the disruption was due to an "extraordinary circumstance."** Airlines often misclassify issues.
> - **Use [EU Claim Form](https://ec.europa.eu/transport/passenger-rights/en)** to file complaints.

---
2.2. UNITED STATES (DOT RULES)
---
**Applies to:**
- All flights **operating to/from/within the USA**.

**Compensation Eligibility:**
| Disruption Type       | Compensation? | Notes                                  |
|-----------------------|---------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Overbooking**       | Yes           | Up to $1,350 (based on delay length). |
| **Delays**            | No            | Only if passenger is bumped.          |
| **Cancellations**     | No            | Refund required if >14 days notice.  |

**Denied Boarding Compensation (2026):**
| Delay Length          | Compensation ($) |
|-----------------------|------------------|
| 1–2 hours             | 200–400          |
| >2 hours (domestic)   | 400–675          |
| >2 hours (int’l)      | 675–1,350        |

**Additional Rights:**
- **Refunds:** Mandatory if flight is canceled >14 days before departure.
- **Re-routing:** Airline must offer alternative transport.

> **Real-Life Scenario: US Overbooking Compensation**
> **Situation:** A passenger was denied boarding on a **Delta flight from JFK to LAX** due to overbooking. The next flight was **3 hours later**.
> **Compensation:** **$675** (since the delay was >2 hours on a domestic flight).
> **Lesson:** **Always ask for the maximum compensation**—airlines often start with lower offers.

2.3. CANADA (APPR)
---
**Applies to:**
- All flights **to/from/within Canada** on airlines with **>1M passengers/year**.

**Compensation Eligibility:**
| Disruption Type       | Delay Duration | Compensation (CAD) | Notes                                  |
|-----------------------|-----------------|--------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Delay**             | 3–6 hours       | 125–400            | Based on delay length.                |
| **Cancellation**      | <14 days notice | 125–1,000          | Unless safety-related.                 |
| **Denied Boarding**   | Any             | 900–2,400          | Based on delay length.                |

**Distance-Based Compensation for Cancellations:**
| Flight Distance       | Compensation (CAD) |
|-----------------------|--------------------|
| <1,500 km             | 125–400             |
| 1,500–3,500 km        | 250–700             |
| >3,500 km             | 500–1,000           |

> **Pro Tip:**
> - **Canada’s rules are stricter than the US**—passengers can claim for **delays as short as 3 hours**.
> - **File claims via the [Canadian Transportation Agency](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/)**.

**Compensation Eligibility:**

**Assistance Rights:**
- **Meals/Refreshments:** For delays >2 hours.
- **Accommodation:** If overnight delay.
- **Re-routing:** Alternative flight or refund.

> **Real-Life Scenario: Qantas Cancellation**
> **Situation:** A Qantas flight from **Sydney to Melbourne** was canceled due to "operational issues." The passenger was rebooked **8 hours later**.
> **Compensation:** **AUD$400** (as the delay was >3 hours and Qantas’s fault).
> **Lesson:** **Always ask for written confirmation of the reason**—if it’s "operational," compensation is due.

3. TYPES OF FLIGHT DISRUPTIONS
===============================================================================
---
3.1. DELAYS
---
**Definition:** Flight departs **later than scheduled**.
**Compensation Eligibility:**
- **EU:** 3+ hours delay = €250–€600.
- **US:** No compensation (unless denied boarding).
- **Canada:** 3+ hours = CAD$125–$400.

**Common Causes:**
| Cause               | Compensation? | Notes                                  |
|---------------------|---------------|----------------------------------------|
| Crew Shortage       | Yes           | Airline’s operational issue.          |
| Technical Problems  | Sometimes     | If due to poor maintenance = Yes.    |
| Weather             | No            | "Extraordinary circumstance."         |
| Air Traffic Control | No            | Out of airline’s control.             |
| Strike (Airline)    | Yes (EU)      | No (US/Canada).                       |

**Agent Action Plan:**
1. **Verify the reason** for the delay (ask airline staff for written confirmation).
2. **Check eligibility** based on departure country and airline.
3. **Document everything** (photos of departure boards, emails from airline).
4. **Demand assistance** (meals, hotel, transport) if delay >2 hours (EU) or overnight.

---
3.2. CANCELLATIONS
---
**Definition:** Flight is **completely canceled** (not just delayed).
**Compensation Eligibility:**
- **EU:** €250–€600 if <14 days notice (unless "extraordinary circumstances").
- **US:** Refund only (no compensation unless overbooking).
- **Canada:** CAD$125–$1,000.

**Common Causes:**
| Cause               | Compensation? | Notes                                  |
|---------------------|---------------|----------------------------------------|
| Low Demand          | Yes           | Airline’s commercial decision.        |
| Operational Issues  | Yes           | Crew shortage, technical problems.    |
| Weather             | No            | "Extraordinary circumstance."         |
| Strike (Airline)    | Yes (EU)      | No (US/Canada).                       |
| Government Restrictions | No         | E.g., COVID-19 travel bans.          |

**Agent Action Plan:**
1. **Demand a refund** if passenger no longer wishes to travel.
2. **Request re-routing** on the next available flight (even with another airline).
3. **File for compensation** if cancellation was <14 days notice and not due to "extraordinary circumstances."

---
3.3. DENIED BOARDING (OVERBOOKING)
---
**Definition:** Passenger with a **valid ticket** is **not allowed to board** due to overbooking.
**Compensation Eligibility:**
- **Always compensable** in EU/US/Canada.
- **Volunteers** (who give up seats for vouchers) are **not eligible** for legal compensation.

**Compensation Amounts:**
| Region       | Compensation Range       | Notes                                  |
|--------------|--------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **EU**       | €250–€600                | Based on distance.                    |
| **US**       | $200–$1,350              | Based on delay length.                |
| **Canada**   | CAD$900–$2,400           | Based on delay length.                |

**Agent Action Plan:**
1. **Ask for written confirmation** of denied boarding.
2. **Negotiate cash compensation** (not just vouchers).
3. **File a claim** if the airline offers less than the legal minimum.

> **Real-Life Scenario: United Overbooking**
> **Situation:** A passenger was denied boarding on **United Airlines** due to overbooking. The next flight was **4 hours later**.
> **Compensation:** **$675** (as the delay was >2 hours on a domestic flight).
> **Lesson:** **Always ask for cash**—airlines prefer vouchers, but passengers have the right to cash.

---
3.4. MISSED CONNECTIONS
---
**Definition:** Passenger **misses a connecting flight** due to a delay on the first leg.
**Compensation Eligibility:**
- **EU:** If both flights are on the **same ticket**, the airline must provide **alternative transport + compensation** if the delay is their fault.
- **US/Canada:** No compensation, but airline must **rebook or refund**.

**Common Causes:**
| Cause               | Airline Responsibility? | Notes                                  |
|---------------------|--------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Delay on First Leg  | Yes                      | If due to airline’s fault.             |
| Short Layover       | Sometimes                | If layover was unreasonably short.     |
| Airport Chaos      | No                       | E.g., security lines, weather.        |

**Agent Action Plan:**
1. **Demand re-routing** on the next available flight (even with a partner airline).
2. **Request meals/hotel** if the wait is >4 hours.
3. **File for compensation** if the missed connection was due to an airline-caused delay on the first leg.

---
3.5. DIVERSIONS
---
**Definition:** Flight lands at an **unplanned airport** due to emergency (e.g., medical, weather, technical).
**Compensation Eligibility:**
- **No compensation** if due to safety/emergency.
- **Assistance required** (meals, hotel, transport to original destination).

**Agent Action Plan:**
1. **Confirm the reason** for diversion (safety vs. operational).
2. **Demand assistance** (hotel, meals, transport).
3. **If operational issue**, file for compensation (rare, but possible in EU).

---
3.6. TECHNICAL ISSUES
---
**Definition:** Flight delayed/canceled due to **mechanical or technical problems**.
**Compensation Eligibility:**
- **Yes**, unless it’s a **safety-related defect** (e.g., engine failure mid-flight).
- **Airlines often claim "safety" to avoid compensation**—**challenge this** if the issue was preventable.

**Agent Action Plan:**
1. **Ask for the exact technical issue** (written confirmation).
2. **If it’s a routine maintenance issue**, demand compensation.
3. **If it’s a safety defect**, focus on assistance (hotel/meals).

4. STEP-BY-STEP CLAIM PROCESS
===============================================================================
---
4.1. IMMEDIATE ACTIONS AT THE AIRPORT
---
1. **Get Written Confirmation:**
   - Ask airline staff for a **letter/email** stating:
     - Flight number, date, and **reason for disruption**.
     - Expected delay duration or cancellation notice.
   - **Example:** *"This flight was canceled due to crew shortage"* = **eligible for compensation**.

2. **Collect Evidence:**
   - **Photos:** Departure boards showing delay/cancellation.
   - **Tickets/Boarding Passes:** Proof of booking.
   - **Receipts:** Meals, hotel, transport (if paid out-of-pocket).

3. **Demand Assistance:**
   - **EU/Canada:** Meals, hotel, transport **must be provided** for delays >2–4 hours.
   - **US:** Only if denied boarding (overbooking).

4. **Request Re-routing or Refund:**
   - **Alternative flight** (even with another airline).
   - **Full refund** if no longer wishing to travel.

---
4.2. DOCUMENTING THE DISRUPTION
---
**Checklist for Agents:**
✅ **Flight details** (number, date, route).
✅ **Reason for disruption** (written confirmation from airline).
✅ **Duration of delay** (departure vs. actual time).
✅ **Assistance provided** (meals, hotel, vouchers).
✅ **Expenses incurred** (receipts for meals, transport, hotel).
✅ **Communication records** (emails, chat logs with airline).

> **Pro Tip:**
> - **Use apps like [FlightAware](https://www.flightaware.com)** to track delays and get official records.
> - **Take screenshots** of airline communications (Twitter, email, chat).

---
4.3. FILING A CLAIM WITH THE AIRLINE
---
**Step-by-Step:**
1. **Find the Airline’s Claim Form:**
   - Most airlines have online forms (e.g., [Emirates Claim Form](https://www.emirates.com/fr/french/help/claims/)).
   - **Example URLs:**
     - Emirates: `https://www.emirates.com/fr/french/help/claims/`
     - Delta: `https://www.delta.com/us/en/need-help/claims`
     - Lufthansa: `https://www.lufthansa.com/fr/fr/claims`

2. **Fill Out the Form:**
   - Provide **flight details, reason for disruption, and evidence**.
   - **Demand cash compensation** (not vouchers).

3. **Submit Within Deadline:**
   - **EU:** Up to **3 years** (varies by country).
   - **US/Canada:** **1 year** (check airline policy).

4. **Follow Up:**
   - Airlines have **30–60 days** to respond.
   - **If no response**, escalate to regulatory bodies (see Section 4.4).

**Sample Claim Letter (See Section 13.1).**

4.5. LEGAL RECOURSE (SMALL CLAIMS COURT)
---
**When to Use:**
- Airline **refuses to pay** despite clear eligibility.
- Claim is **<€5,000 (EU)** or **<$10,000 (US/Canada)**.

**Steps:**
1. **Send a final demand letter** to the airline (see Section 13.2).
2. **File in small claims court** in the airline’s home country (e.g., **Dublin for Ryanair**).
3. **Use services like [AirHelp](https://www.airhelp.com/)** or **local lawyers** specializing in flight claims.

> **Real-Life Scenario: Ryanair Refusal**
> **Situation:** Ryanair refused to pay **€600 compensation** for a 6-hour delay (crew shortage).
> **Solution:**
> 1. Passenger filed a complaint with the **Irish Aviation Authority**.
> 2. **Ryanair paid within 30 days** after regulatory intervention.
> **Lesson:** **Regulatory complaints work**—use them if airlines refuse.

6. REAL-LIFE SCENARIOS & SOLUTIONS
===============================================================================
---
6.1. DELAYED FLIGHT DUE TO STRIKE (EU)
---
**Situation:**
- **Flight:** Paris (CDG) to Barcelona (BCN) on **Air France**.
- **Reason:** Air France **crew strike**.
- **Delay:** **5 hours**.

**Solution:**
1. **Demand written confirmation** from Air France (strike = **not an extraordinary circumstance** in EU).
2. **Claim €400 compensation** (flight distance: 1,000 km).
3. **Receive meal vouchers** (€20) and **hotel** (overnight stay).
4. **File claim via [Air France’s form](https://www.airfrance.fr/)**.
5. **Receive €400 within 30 days**.

**Lesson:**
- **Strikes by airline staff are compensable in the EU** (unlike US/Canada).
- **Always get the reason in writing**—airlines may misclassify strikes as "extraordinary."

---
6.2. CANCELLATION DUE TO WEATHER (US)
---
**Situation:**
- **Flight:** New York (JFK) to Miami (MIA) on **Delta**.
- **Reason:** **Hurricane warning**.
- **Notice:** **24 hours before departure**.

**Solution:**
1. **No compensation** (weather = extraordinary circumstance).
2. **Demand a full refund** (DOT rules).
3. **Re-book on another airline** (Delta must refund if passenger chooses not to travel).
4. **Use travel insurance** to cover hotel/meals during delay.

**Lesson:**
- **Weather cancellations = no compensation**, but **refunds are mandatory** in the US.
- **Travel insurance** can cover additional costs (hotel, meals).

---
6.3. DENIED BOARDING (OVERBOOKING IN CANADA)
---
**Situation:**
- **Flight:** Toronto (YYZ) to Vancouver (YVR) on **Air Canada**.
- **Reason:** **Overbooking**.
- **Next flight:** **6 hours later**.

**Solution:**
1. **Demand cash compensation** (CAD$900–$1,800 for 6-hour delay).
2. **Air Canada offers $600 voucher**—**reject and insist on cash**.
3. **File claim via [CTA](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/)**.
4. **Receive $1,200** (as per APPR rules).

**Lesson:**
- **In Canada, passengers can reject vouchers** and demand **cash compensation**.
- **Always check the APPR rules**—they’re stricter than US/EU in some cases.

2. **File claim with Emirates** for the delay + missed connection.

**Lesson:**
